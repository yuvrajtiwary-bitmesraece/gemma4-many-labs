## P3. Replicate noise and detectable differences

- 127 pairs of repeated runs, 49 systems, 9 labs; median 26 shared tasks per pair.
- **Flip rate** (share of shared tasks whose outcome differs between two repeats): pooled **10.9%**; across labs median 10.4%, interquartile range 10.0% to 12.5%, range 0.0% to 16.7%.
- Absolute difference in resolve rate between two repeats: pooled 3.3 points; across labs median 6.0 points, range 0.0 to 16.7 points; largest single pair 25.0 points; 14.2% of pairs had identical outcomes on every task.
- Minimum detectable improvement for one paired comparison (exact two-sided McNemar test, α = 0.05, power 0.80, discordance = flip rate + improvement): **34.5 points** on 30 tasks, **20.8 points** on 60 tasks, **12.5 points** on 129 tasks.
- Consistency check fixed in advance: pooled flip rate 10.9% against twice the replicate-noise variance 10.7% (unweighted).

*(review)* What the planned numbers above do and do not say:

- **Weight.** The pooled rate is dominated by two labs (86% of the weight; the effective number of labs is 2.6 of 9) and one system with 6 runs carries 31%. Other ways of averaging the same pairs give: each system once 10.8%; first pair of each system 10.4%; mean over systems 10.6%; each of the 124 tasks with repeats once 11.4%; mean over labs 10.4%. All lie between 10.4% and 11.4%.
- **Coverage.** No pair of repeats covers all 129 tasks; the largest covers 113 and 19 pairs cover at least 100 (in 1 lab). The rate is measured on the subsets labs chose for repeats and applied to 129 tasks; counting each task once (11.4%) supports that step.
- **What a flip is.** In 51% of the 589 flips the failing attempt carries an error flag (a timeout, an exhausted budget or a tool error). Where neither attempt is flagged the flip rate is 7.9%. About half of the run-to-run noise is therefore an agent that sometimes finishes in time and sometimes does not.
- **The detectable difference depends on how an improvement is spread over tasks.** The planned calculation assumes the worst case for a paired test: every gained task changes from never solved to always solved, so a gain adds its whole size to the disagreement between the two systems. If a gain added no disagreement (for example because it makes unstable tasks reliable), the same test would detect 8.2 points on 129 tasks. In this pool, 26 pairs of different systems of one lab that both have repeats (3 labs; differences of at most 6.6 points) disagree on 11.3% of tasks against 9.5% between repeats of one system: 1.8 points more, which gives **9.0 points**. So one paired comparison on 129 tasks detects a true difference of roughly 8.2 to 12.5 points; the planned figure is the upper end. The within-lab variance components of P2b imply 8.6 points (median of the labs), in the same range.

| Tasks | Planned (gain adds its size to the disagreement) | Disagreement as observed between systems | Gain adds no disagreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 34.5 points | 25.2 points | 25.2 points |
| 60 | 20.8 points | 13.0 points | 13.0 points |
| 129 | 12.5 points | 9.0 points | 8.2 points |

The planned values are found on a grid of 0.25 points; on a grid of 0.05 points they are 34.45, 20.60, 12.30 points.

## P4. Subset comparability

- Rasch model on 259 systems and 129 tasks (converged: True). Reference system: median ability among the 193 systems labelled 31B.
- Runs used **52** distinct task subsets of at least 10 tasks. Predicted resolve rate of the reference system: **5.6% to 66.7%** (a spread of 61.1 points; interquartile range 30.2% to 38.5%); on the full task set 32.9%.
- Restricted to the 22 subsets of at least 30 tasks: 25.5% to 57.9%.
- *(review)* Every one of the 52 subsets is used by one lab only (0 are shared), and 6 of them were chosen by the lab from the outcomes of its earlier runs (tasks that had failed, tasks that had flipped, or tasks already solved). The lowest and the highest prediction are both such subsets. Without them the range over the remaining 46 subsets is **17.7% to 64.4%**; over the 21 of at least 30 tasks, **25.5% to 39.3%**; over the 11 subsets of at least 50 tasks, 26.7% to 36.7%.
- *(review)* Model check, out of sample (5-fold over the 12,019 system × task cells): Brier score 0.074 on held-out cells, against 0.105 for task means alone and 0.189 for the base rate (0.065 in sample); per run, predicted against observed resolve rate on held-out cells: Pearson 0.986, mean absolute error 1.9 points; largest gap between predicted and observed rate over the ten deciles of predicted probability 1.9 points. (The in-sample check reported before Amendment 4 was empty: the model's score equations force predicted and observed totals per system to agree.)
- *(review)* Does the model predict the *subset effect* for data it has not seen? For each of the 35 systems that attempted at least 85 tasks (3 labs) and each subset, task difficulties were fitted without that system's lab and the system's ability on its tasks outside the subset; the model then predicted its rate inside the subset (1,660 system × subset pairs). Mean absolute error 6.3 points, against 12.1 points for the assumption that a system scores the same on any subset; predicted and observed subset effects correlate at 0.87 with a slope of 1.03.

## Sensitivity and uncertainty (added after the first results; not in the plan)

Each line changes one decision or definition and recomputes the affected quantity (`scripts/sensitivity.py`, `results/sensitivity.json`).

- **Uncertainty of the flip rate** *(review)*. Counting every lab once, the mean of the 9 lab-level flip rates is **10.4%** with a 95% t interval of **7.0% to 13.9%**. The pooled rate (10.9%) is dominated by two labs (86% of the weight; effective number of labs 2.6), so intervals for it are too narrow: lab-cluster bootstrap 8.4% to 12.3%, system-cluster bootstrap 8.5% to 12.5%, delete-one-lab jackknife 7.2% to 14.5%. In a simulation with the observed lab sizes and labs that differ by 2 to 4 points, the nominal 95% intervals for the pooled rate cover the truth 84% to 86% (bootstrap) and 83% to 84% (jackknife) of the time; the interval on the lab-level rates covers 94% to 96%. Leaving out one lab at a time moves the pooled rate between 9.4% and 11.7%. At the ends of the lab-level interval the planned detectable improvement on 129 tasks is 10.8 points and 13.5 points.
- **Repeats only on the strongest evidence.** Splitting every group of repeats that an independent audit graded as resting on weaker evidence (in L01 one group pooled in the lab's reporting but never called repeats; in L02 one pair that shares only a label and one group pooled across two machines; 13 runs): 110 pairs, pooled flip rate 10.2%, lab median 10.6%.
- **Without repeats on task subsets that a lab chose from earlier outcomes** (such subsets are picked for unstable, failed or already solved tasks; 18 runs are on such subsets, 3 pairs of repeats among them, with a flip rate of 26.6%): 124 pairs in 9 labs, pooled flip rate 10.6%, lab median 10.0%, interquartile range 9.2% to 10.4%, largest lab 16.7%.

## A noise band for a new lab (added after the results; not in the plan)

Can the pooled data tell a team how far two runs of one agent will differ on its own task subset, before it has run a repeat? Model (`scripts/noise_model.py`): task difficulties from the pooled Rasch fit; the agent's ability set from its score on its tasks; two runs disagree on a task with probability 2p(1-p), scaled by one constant because real agents are more consistent than a one-parameter model implies.

- Scale: observed flips are **0.78** of the unscaled prediction (589 observed in 127 pairs); leave-one-lab-out it ranges from 0.74 to 0.80.
- **Leave one lab out** (difficulties and scale from the other labs, checked on the held-out lab's repeats): predicted 599 flips against 589 observed; correlation of predicted and observed flips across pairs 0.93; the 95% band for the score difference of two repeats covers **96.1%** of the 127 held-out pairs (mean over labs 97.6%); mean absolute score difference 4.6 points observed, 5.0 points predicted.
- **External dataset** (band from the pool only; other model sizes, temperature 1.0): the 95% band covers 95.7% of its 69 pairs; observed flips are 1.13 times the prediction; mean absolute score difference 2.5 points observed, 2.7 points predicted.
- `tools/compare_runs.py` applies the band to two sets of result files and says whether their difference is inside it. Beside it, it gives an exact test that uses no model (the exact McNemar test for one run each, the exact permutation test within tasks for several runs each) and the number of runs a given gain needs on those tasks. `scripts/test_compare_runs.py` checks it against the pooled repeats.
- **A worked example from the pool** (two configurations of one lab, L02, both run four times on the same 61 tasks; they differ in the thinking budget). One run each: 31.1% against 37.7%, a difference of 6.6 points, inside the band of ±8.3 points (exact McNemar p = 0.29). Three runs each: 6.6 points, outside the band of ±4.8 points (exact test within tasks p = 0.020). Four runs each: 6.1 points, band ±4.2 points, p = 0.011. With three runs the difference is clear; one run of each could not show it.
